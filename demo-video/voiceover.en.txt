Engineering projects often collect scattered files: parts lists, notes, review logs, and claims.
Before a handoff, the practical question is simple: what is actually backed by evidence?

This is UAV Readiness & Evidence Copilot, a safe documentation QA demo.
It is not a drone control tool and it does not work with live aircraft data.

The demo page shows a documentation readiness score, plus evidence counters for verified, partial, and locked items.
Verified means there is proof. Partial means the proof is incomplete. Locked means the tool refuses to approve the item because evidence is missing.

Behind the page, a TypeScript CLI reads synthetic demo artifacts, builds an evidence graph, runs simple readiness rules, and generates useful handoff files.
Those outputs include a markdown report, evidence graph JSON, traceability CSV, artifact hashes, and a portfolio demo page.

The value is safe engineering QA: evidence tracking, traceability, and readiness reporting without operational UAV features.
For a recruiter or engineering team, this shows practical internal-tool thinking, safety boundaries, and testable TypeScript implementation.
